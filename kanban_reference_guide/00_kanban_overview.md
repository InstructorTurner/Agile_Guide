# 🧭 00: Kanban Overview - The Philosophy of Flow

**Goal:** To introduce the core philosophy of Kanban and explain how its focus on "flow" differs from the "time-boxed" approach of Scrum.

## 🌊 What is Kanban?
Kanban (Japanese for "visual signal") is a method for managing work by visualizing the workflow, limiting work in progress, and focusing on the continuous delivery of value.

Unlike Scrum, which organizes work into fixed-length Sprints, Kanban is a **continuous flow system**. Work is pulled through the system as capacity becomes available, rather than being committed to in a batch at the start of a cycle.

## ⚖️ Kanban vs. Scrum: The Core Difference

| Feature | Scrum | Kanban |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cadence** | Regular Sprints (1-4 weeks) | Continuous Flow |
| **Commitment** | Sprint Goal & Backlog | No fixed commitment; focus on Throughput |
| **Change** | Scope is protected during Sprint | Changes can be made to the backlog at any time |
| **Key Metric** | Velocity (Story Points per Sprint) | Cycle Time & Throughput |

**💡 Why learn this if we use Scrum?**
Even in a Scrum environment, Kanban concepts—like **limiting WIP** and **identifying bottlenecks**—are incredibly powerful. By applying "flow thinking" to a Sprint, a team can reduce the "end-of-sprint crunch" and deliver a more stable product.
