# 🔄 Sprint Boards

The Sprint Board (or Taskboard) is where the team manages the "now." While the Backlog is for the future, the Sprint Board is for the current iteration.

## Accessing the Board

Navigate to **Boards > Sprints**. By default, this opens the **Taskboard** view.

## Best Practices for Junior Developers

### 1. Keeping the Board Current
The board is our primary communication tool. If a task is "In Progress" on the board but you finished it two hours ago, the board is lying to the team.

*   **Action**: Move your card to "Done" the moment the work is verified.
*   **Action**: Update your "Remaining Work" hours daily so the Burndown chart stays accurate.

### 2. Managing Your Tasks
When you start a task, drag it from the **To Do** column to the **In Progress** column. 

*   **Tip**: Only have one task "In Progress" at a time. This follows the Agile principle of "Stop Starting, Start Finishing."

### 3. The Daily Scrum Integration
During the Daily Scrum, we use the Sprint Board as our visual anchor. Instead of just saying what you did, refer to the card.

*Example:* "I'm currently working on Task #452 (the API integration). I've moved it to In Progress and expect to move it to Done by tomorrow afternoon."

## 💡 Narrative Example: The "Stuck" Card

Imagine you've been working on a task for two days, and it's still in the **In Progress** column. Your Scrum Master notices this during the daily sync. 

Because the card is visible, the team can immediately ask: "Is there a blocker we can help remove?" This prevents you from struggling in silence and ensures the team hits the sprint goal together.

## ⚙️ Pro Tip: Capacity Planning

In the **Boards > Sprints** view, there is a **Capacity** tab at the top. 

This is where you tell ADO your availability for the sprint. You can set your "Days Off" (vacations/holidays) and your "Capacity per day" (how many hours you can actually spend on tasks).

**Why this matters:**
When you assign tasks to yourself, ADO calculates your total workload. If you are over-capacity, your workload bar on the Taskboard will turn red. This is a signal to the team that you have too much on your plate and some tasks may need to be redistributed.
