# 📦 Backlog Management

The Backlog is the source of truth for all planned work. Managing it correctly ensures that the team always knows what the highest priority item is.

## Accessing the Backlog

Navigate to **Boards > Backlogs**. Here you will see a flat list of User Stories (and potentially Features/Epics depending on your view).

## Key Interactions

### 1. Refining the Story
As a developer, you aren't just a "ticket taker." During refinement, you should look at the stories in the backlog and ask questions.

*   **Check the Acceptance Criteria**: Does the story clearly define when it is "Done"? If not, ask the Product Owner (PO) for clarification.
*   **Break Down Stories**: If a story feels too large to fit in a sprint, suggest splitting it into two smaller stories.

### 2. Moving Work to the Sprint
During Sprint Planning, the team drags items from the **Backlog** view into the **Current Sprint**.

*   **Best Practice**: Ensure every story moved into the sprint is "Ready" (has clear criteria and an estimate).

## 🛠️ ADO Menu Tips

In the **Boards > Backlogs** view, use the **Filtering** icon (the funnel) to find stories assigned to you or stories with specific tags (e.g., "Technical Debt"). This helps you quickly identify work you are uniquely qualified to handle.

## 💡 Narrative Example: The "Vague" Story

You see a story in the backlog: "Improve performance of the search page." 

This is too vague to be "Ready." Instead of waiting for the sprint to start, you comment on the work item in ADO: *"Could we specify a target load time? For example, 'Search results should load in under 2 seconds for 1000 records'."* 

By doing this in the backlog, you prevent a "blocker" from occurring mid-sprint.
