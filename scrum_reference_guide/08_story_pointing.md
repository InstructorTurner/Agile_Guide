# 🔢 08: Story Pointing - Estimating Effort

**Goal:** To provide a toolkit for the team to size work based on effort, complexity, and uncertainty, rather than hours.

## 📖 What are Story Points?
Story points are a unit of measure for expressing an overall estimate of the amount of work involved in implementing a User Story. Unlike hours, points represent **relative sizing**.

**Why avoid hours?**
*   **Individual Velocity:** A senior developer might finish a task in 2 hours that takes a junior developer 8 hours. The *complexity* of the task remains the same regardless of who does it.
*   **The "Estimation Trap":** Hour-based estimates are often treated as deadlines. Points focus on the effort and risk involved.

## 🛠️ Estimation Techniques

Depending on the stage of planning or the complexity of the task, the team can use different methods:

1.  **Fibonacci Sequence (1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21):** 
    *   Used to account for uncertainty. The gap between numbers increases as the story gets larger, reflecting the fact that larger stories are harder to estimate accurately.
2.  **Planning Poker:** 
    *   A ritual where team members simultaneously reveal their point estimate (using cards or software). This prevents "anchoring," where junior members simply agree with the first number mentioned by a senior.
3.  **T-Shirt Sizing (XS, S, M, L, XL):** 
    *   Ideal for high-level, early-stage estimation (e.g., during backlog grooming) when the team doesn't need precise points but needs to know if a feature is "Small" or "Extra Large."
4.  **The "Quick Sync":** 
    *   For simple, well-understood tasks, the team can skip the formal poker process and use a brief conversation to reach a consensus quickly.

## 📈 Velocity & Future Planning

The true power of story points is realized over time through **Velocity**.

*   **Tracking Velocity:** Velocity is the sum of all story points completed in a single sprint. 
*   **Informing Decisions:** By averaging velocity over 3-4 sprints, the team can predictably forecast how much work they can realistically commit to in the next sprint.
*   **Capacity Planning:** If the team's average velocity is 30 points, and the Product Owner wants to pull in a 40-point set of stories, the team knows they are over-committing and can negotiate the scope.

**The Golden Rule:** Points are a team agreement used for forecasting, not a management quota for measuring individual performance.
