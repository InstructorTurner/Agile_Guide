# 🔄 06: Sprint Retrospective - How We Get Better

**Goal:** To create a safe, blame-free space for the team to inspect *our process* and identify improvements for the next Sprint.

## 💬 What is it?
Unlike the Sprint Review (where we look at the *Product*), the Retrospective looks at the *Process*. It is a dedicated meeting to ask: **What went well? What slowed us down? What can we improve?**

**💡 Retrospective vs. Post-Mortem:**
If you are used to Waterfall "Post-Mortems," note the difference:
*   **Post-Mortem:** Happens at the end of a project; often focused on what went wrong and who was responsible.
*   **Retrospective:** Happens every few weeks; focused on how the *process* can evolve to make the team more successful. It is about growth, not blame.

## ⚙️ The Format: Safe and Structured
The meeting must be structured to ensure balanced feedback. Common frameworks include:

1.  **What Went Well:** What should we continue doing? (Keep doing!)
2.  **What Could Be Improved:** What added friction or slowed us down? (Stop doing!)
3.  **Action Items:** The most important part. By the end of the meeting, we must agree on **1-3 concrete, assignable, and measurable actions** that will be implemented in the *next* Sprint.

**🔄 Closing the Loop:** These action items are not "suggestions"—they are commitments. They should be added to the next Sprint Backlog as actual tasks to ensure they aren't forgotten. At the start of the *following* Retro, we always ask: *"Did we actually do the action items we committed to last time?"*

### 🧠 Key Rules for Success
*   **No Blame:** The focus is always on the *process*, not the *person*. (e.g., "Our testing cycle was too slow," not "John tested slowly.")
*   **Actionable:** If we identify a problem without assigning an owner or a timeframe for solving it, we haven't run a Retrospective—we've just complained.
