## Agile Manifesto Overview

This guide serves as a foundational resource detailing the core principles and practices of Agile methodologies. While other frameworks (like Scrum and Kanban) provide specific rulesets, Agile is the underlying philosophy—a mindset shift—that guides how we build software and deliver value.

### What is Agile?

At its heart, Agile is an iterative and adaptive approach to project management and software development. Instead of planning a project from start to finish (a traditional, "waterfall" approach), Agile embraces the reality that requirements *will* change and that timely feedback is the most valuable asset.

**Goal:** To deliver working, valuable software increments frequently and continuously, adapting to change rather than resisting it.

### The Core Principles

Agile methodologies are underpinned by the **Agile Manifesto**, created in 2001. It is not a rigid process, but a commitment to a mindset focused on:

1.  **Individuals and Interactions:** Prioritizing communication and collaboration over strict processes.
2.  **Working Software:** Delivering functional, testable software increments over extensive documentation.
3.  **Customer Collaboration:** Working closely with stakeholders and end-users throughout the entire development life cycle.
4.  **Responding to Change:** Embracing changing requirements at any point in the project lifecycle, often viewed as a benefit rather than a disruptive cost.

### Agile vs. Waterfall (A Quick Contrast)

*   **Waterfall:** Requires all requirements to be defined upfront. Changes are expensive and difficult to integrate later on. Progress is linear.
*   **Agile:** Assumes requirements will evolve. It breaks work into small, manageable cycles (iterations), allowing for course correction and early validation of assumptions. Progress is cyclical.
