# 01 Human-First Breakdown

[← Back to Index](index.md)

The most dangerous moment in AI-assisted development is the transition from a raw idea to an AI prompt. If you let the LLM handle the breakdown of requirements, you risk "cognitive outsourcing"—where you stop thinking about the edge cases because the AI has provided a plausible-looking list.

## The Brain-First Rule

**Break down your concepts by hand before turning to an LLM.**

When you do the breakdown yourself, you are forced to encounter the "friction" of the problem. This friction is where the actual engineering happens. 

### How to Breakdown By-Hand

Avoid using complex templates; instead, focus on these three questions:
1. **What is the core value?** (What is the one thing this *must* do?)
2. **What are the boundaries?** (What will this *not* do?)
3. **What are the "hidden" steps?** (What happens between the user clicking a button and the data appearing on screen?)

## Why This Matters

LLMs are designed to be helpful and agreeable. If you ask an LLM to "break down the requirements for a photo album app," it will give you a generic, polished list. Because the list is polished, your brain may subconsciously signal that the problem is "solved," causing you to miss critical project-specific needs.

By writing the breakdown yourself—even in rough bullet points or a physical sketch—you maintain ownership of the logic. You enter the AI conversation as a **reviewer** and **director**, rather than a passive consumer of AI-generated suggestions.
