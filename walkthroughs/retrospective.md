# 🔄 Sprint Retrospective Walkthrough

Your mission today is to move from "venting about the sprint" to contributing a concrete process improvement that makes the next sprint better.

## 🎯 Final Deliverable
By the end of the session, the team must have:

1. At least **one concrete process change** committed to the next Sprint Backlog.
2. Your personal contribution of at least one "Keep" and one "Improve" observation.

---

## 🛠️ The Workflow

### Phase 1: The Reflection (Pre-Work)
Avoid "blank page syndrome" by reviewing your sprint before the meeting starts.

- **Activity:** Scan your recent commits, PR comments, and any tickets where you were blocked.
- **Identify Friction:** Look for patterns. 
  - *Bad:* "I was stuck on that one bug for three days."
  - *Better:* "I spent three days on one bug because I didn't have a local environment setup for that module" -> **Observation:** Our onboarding/setup docs are outdated.

### Phase 2: The Contribution (The Meeting)
Contribute high-value feedback that helps the team grow without creating conflict.

1. **The "Keep" Card:** Recognize a win. Credit a teammate's help, a new tool, or a communication habit that worked.
   - *Example:* "The mid-sprint sync on Tuesday really helped clear up the API confusion."
2. **The "Improve" Card:** Apply the **Process over Person** rule. Convert a complaint into a process observation.
   - *Wrong:* "The Product Owner didn't give us clear requirements."
   - *Right:* "The User Stories lacked clear Acceptance Criteria, which led to rework."

### Phase 3: The Pivot (Action Item Generation)
Help the team move from identifying a problem to solving it.

- **Suggest an Experiment:** Instead of demanding a permanent change, suggest a trial for the next sprint.
- **The Formula:** "What if we tried [Action] for the next sprint to see if it solves [Problem]?"
- **Example:** "What if we tried a 15-minute 'PR Power Hour' every afternoon to reduce turnaround time?"

---

## ✅ Definition of a "Good Contribution" (Checklist)
Before you post your card or speak up, audit your feedback:

- [ ] Does it focus on the *process*, not the *person*?
- [ ] Is it specific (referencing a real event from the sprint)?
- [ ] Does it offer a potential path toward a solution?
- [ ] Is it honest but constructive?
